Structure of Logical Statements - Lecture 13

A well-formed formula (wff) is built from propositional symbols (p, q, r...), connectives (¬ ∧ ∨ ⇒ ⇔) and brackets

  • Every symbol on its own is a wff
  • If p and q are wffs, so are (¬p), (p ∧ q), (p ∨ q), (p ⇒ q), (p ⇔ q)
  • Nothing else is a wff

Draw a structure tree: the main connective is the root, ¬ has 1 branch, the others have 2, symbols are the leaves, and brackets never appear

Dropping brackets: apply connectives in the order ¬, ∧, ∨, ⇒, ⇔

Use the tree to build big truth tables, one column per sub-formula

Logical Equivalence and Satisfiability - Lecture 14

Two formulas are equivalent (≡) if they have the same truth value for every assignment

  • De Morgan: ¬(p ∧ q) ≡ ¬p ∨ ¬q, and ¬(p ∨ q) ≡ ¬p ∧ ¬q
  • p ⇒ q ≡ ¬p ∨ q
  • ¬(¬p) ≡ p
  • Distributive: p ∧ (q ∨ r) ≡ (p ∧ q) ∨ (p ∧ r)
  • Tautology - always true, e.g. p ∨ ¬p
  • Contradiction - always false, e.g. p ∧ ¬p
  • Satisfiable - true for at least one assignment

≡ isn't a connective, it's a statement about two formulas
