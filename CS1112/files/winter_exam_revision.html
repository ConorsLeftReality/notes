Sets and Functions

See Lectures 3 and 4 and Lectures 5 and 6

  • Order and repeats don't matter in a set. {} and {∅} are different
  • A ∪ B (either), A ∩ B (both), A \ B (in A not B), A' = U \ A
  • |P(A)| = 2|A|
  • Partition: non-empty, non-overlapping subsets that cover the whole set
  • De Morgan: (A ∪ B)' = A' ∩ B' and (A ∩ B)' = A' ∪ B'
  • Functions: injective (no two inputs share an output), surjective (every output is hit), bijective (both)

Relations

See Lectures 7 and 8 and Lectures 9 and 10

  • A relation is a subset of A × B
  • Reflexive (every x relates to itself), symmetric (xRy means yRx), antisymmetric (xRy and yRx means x = y), transitive (xRy and yRz means xRz)
  • Equivalence relation = reflexive + symmetric + transitive. Its classes partition the set
  • Partial order = reflexive + antisymmetric + transitive

Logic

See Lectures 11 and 12, Lectures 13 and 14 and Lectures 15 and 16

  • p → q is only false when p is true and q is false
  • p → q ≡ ¬p ∨ q ≡ ¬q → ¬p (contrapositive)
  • Tautology (always true), contradiction (always false). Prove equivalence with a truth table
  • An argument is valid if the conclusion is true whenever all the premises are true
  • Bad arguments: affirming the consequent (p → q, q, so p) and denying the antecedent (p → q, ¬p, so ¬q)
