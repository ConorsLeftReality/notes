BRDs and FRDs

Before coding, you need to know what you're actually trying to do

BRD - Business Requirements Document

Defines what the business needs a project to achieve and why. Written by a Business Analyst or Project Manager, from the business's point of view, not the programmer's

Includes: executive summary, objectives, scope, stakeholders, high-level requirements, constraints and assumptions, cost-benefit analysis

E.G. "The business needs a system that allows staff to manage product inventory and monitor stock levels"

FRD - Functional Requirements Document

Turns the business requirements into the specific functions the software must do

Describes: each function, what the user enters, what the system displays, business rules and calculations, how it reacts to different situations, and acceptance criteria (one way to write them is GIVEN ... WHEN ... THEN ...)

E.G. from the inventory BRD:

  1. Display all products
  2. Search for a product by ID
  3. Add new products
  4. Update prices
  5. Update stock quantities
  6. Find products that need reordering

BRD = business needs, what the business wants, high level. FRD = software functionality, what the app must do, detailed

Pair Programming

Two developers. One computer. One shared goal.

  • Driver - on the keyboard, writes the code, implements the agreed solution
  • Navigator - reviews the code, thinks about next steps, spots problems, suggests improvements

The Navigator isn't just watching. The Driver focuses on the code right now, the Navigator on the bigger picture. Both are responsible for the final app

Why

Better problem solving, knowledge sharing, fewer errors, better code quality, communication skills, shared responsibility. It's used a lot in industry

Ground Rules

  • Swap Driver and Navigator regularly (every 15-20 mins)
  • Discuss the solution before writing it
  • Explain your reasoning, don't just tell your partner what to type
  • Don't take over the keyboard
  • Ask if something's unclear, make sure you both understand the code
  • Test it together
