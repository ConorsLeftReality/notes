Specifications to Schemas

Example: the COMPANY database (from Elmasri and Navathe, "Fundamentals of Database Systems"), which keeps track of employees, departments and projects

The requirements description doesn't need to model the whole company, just what needs to go in the database

  • The company is split into departments. Each has a unique name, a unique number, a manager (an employee) with a start date, and maybe several locations
  • A department controls projects, each with a unique name, unique number and one location
  • Each employee has a name, SSN, address, salary, gender and birth date. They're in one department, but can work on several projects (from any department). Track hours per week on each project, and each employee's direct supervisor (another employee)
  • Each employee's dependents (for insurance): first name, sex, birth date, relationship to the employee

Entities

The nouns in the description are usually the entities

  • DEPARTMENT - Name, Number, Locations, Manager, Manager_start_date
  • PROJECT - Name, Number, Location, Controlling_department
  • EMPLOYEE - Name, Ssn, Gender, Address, Salary, Birth_date, Department, Supervisor, Works_on
  • DEPENDENT - Employee, Dependent_name, Gender, Birth_date, Relationship
  • Name and Number of a department are both (separate) key attributes, since each is unique
  • Locations is a multivalued attribute
  • Name and Address might be composite attributes (first/last name etc.), but the requirements don't say, so you'd have to check
  • Works_on could be a multivalued composite attribute (Project, Hours)
Implicit Relationships

Whenever an attribute of one entity refers to another entity (Manager, Controlling_department, Supervisor, Department), there's a relationship hiding in it. In the ER model, make these explicit relationships, not attributes

Relationships

The verbs in the description are usually the relationships. The cardinality comes from the requirements (if it can't, ask the users)

  • MANAGES - 1:1 between EMPLOYEE and DEPARTMENT, with the attribute Start_date on the relationship
  • WORKS_FOR - 1:N between DEPARTMENT and EMPLOYEE
  • CONTROLS - 1:N between DEPARTMENT and PROJECT
  • SUPERVISION - 1:N between EMPLOYEE (supervisor) and EMPLOYEE (supervisee). A reflexive relationship, both ends are the same entity
  • WORKS_ON - M:N between EMPLOYEE and PROJECT, with the attribute Hours
  • DEPENDENTS_OF - 1:N between EMPLOYEE and DEPENDENT. This is the identifying relationship for the weak entity DEPENDENT

The final ER diagram uses (min, max) notation and role names. The multivalued Locations attribute gets replaced with a LOCATIONS entity and relationships HAS_LOCATION (department) and LOCATED_AT (project)
